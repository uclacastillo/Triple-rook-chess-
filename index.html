The official game rules (first version)

How Triple Rook Chess works

  1. Start with a normal 8×8 chessboard and all the usual pieces.
  2. The king is the third rook: it moves any number of squares horizontally or vertically, like a rook. The other two rooks also move normally.
  3. There is no check or checkmate. The king may be captured like any other piece, and that only removes one of your three lives.
  4. Both players secretly select one legal move from the same starting position. Then both moves happen at once. You can also pass.
  5. If both pieces move onto the same destination square, both are removed. If they try to capture each other by swapping squares, both are also removed. Otherwise, a piece that moves away from an attacked square escapes.
  6. The player who loses all three original rooks (the king-rook and two normal rooks) loses. If both players lose their last rook together, it’s a draw.
  7. Pawns can promote, but promoted rooks do not create extra lives. Castling and en passant are not used. Three rounds of both players passing results in a draw.
